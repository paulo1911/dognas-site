Almost everyone has an old phone put away. Throwing it out is a waste and selling it brings little. The good news is that an Android that still turns on and joins Wi-Fi can be repurposed for a lot of things. Here are 10 ideas, with what each one asks of you, so you can pick the one that fits the phone you have.

> [!takeaways]
> - Check the phone first: a **swollen battery** means recycle it, do not use it. An old Android that still charges and joins Wi-Fi is enough for most of these uses.
> - Uses that stay on all day (server, camera, panel, clock) need a **charger and a cool, ventilated spot**.
> - The idea that keeps paying off without you watching it is a **home file server**: it runs quietly on a shelf and keeps your photos, videos and documents at home.
> - Nothing here is a backup. Keep an independent copy of what you cannot lose.

## Before you start: check the phone

Back up what matters and, if the device is going to someone else, reset it to factory settings. Then look at four things:

- **The battery.** If the back is bulging or the case is lifting, stop: do not charge it and take it to a battery recycling point. A healthy battery is fine on a charger, but a phone that stays plugged in all day wears the battery faster, so keep it out of the sun and away from heat.
- **The Android version.** An old version no longer gets security updates. That is fine for a phone that only does one job at home, on your own Wi-Fi. It is a reason not to use it for banking or other sensitive accounts.
- **Storage and memory.** A phone with a few GB free is fine for a clock or a panel, but a file server needs room for the files (or an SD card or USB drive).
- **The charger and the cable.** Uses that stay on need a charger that works well, not a worn cable.

Results depend on the device and the maker: some makers limit background apps to save battery, which can stop an app that has to stay on.

| Webcam | A computer and a cable or Wi-Fi | During use | An app on each side |
| Media hub with a speaker | A speaker | Optional | Almost none |
| Smart home control | A wall mount, your automation app | Yes | Medium |

## 1. Home file server

Turn the device into a place to keep and reach photos, videos and documents over Wi-Fi, with no cloud and no monthly fee for the storage. With **DogNAS**, you install the app, choose where to keep the files (internal storage, an SD card or a USB drive) and connect other phones with a QR Code and a PIN. Computers connect with an SFTP program such as FileZilla.

It is the idea that stays useful every day, because it keeps what is yours and needs no attention. What to know: it is a file server, not a backup and not a RAID, and the speed depends on your Wi-Fi and on the device. See the full walkthrough in [how to turn an old Android into a file server](/blog/turn-old-android-into-file-server/).

## 2. Security camera or baby monitor

With an IP camera app, the phone becomes a camera you watch from another device. It works best with a good camera, strong Wi-Fi and a charger. Keep the stream on your home network and protect the app with a password: do not open the camera to the internet. Pair it with the file server to keep recordings at home, as long as the camera app can send them over a compatible protocol.

## 3. Webcam for your computer

Apps such as DroidCam and Iriun Webcam use the phone's camera as a webcam for meetings and streams, often with better quality than a laptop's. You need the app on the phone and on the computer, and a stable connection by cable or Wi-Fi.

## 4. Media hub with a speaker

Connect the device to a speaker over Bluetooth or a cable and use it as a music player, online radio or podcast player. It is the simplest use of all and it does not need to stay plugged in.

## 5. Smart home control

Mount it on a wall as a panel to control lights and devices from your automation app. It stays on, so it needs a charger and a spot where the screen can stay lit without overheating.

## 6. Router, hotspot or repeater

Android can share its mobile connection as a hotspot, which needs a SIM with a data plan. It does not replace a good router, but it helps in a pinch. As a repeater, results vary a lot by model and app.

## 7. Retro gaming

Emulators such as RetroArch can run classic games with a Bluetooth controller. Use only games you have the right to use. Older phones may run heavier systems slowly.

## 8. Dedicated GPS in the car or on the bike

With downloaded maps and a mount, it navigates without draining your main phone's battery. Keep it out of the sun on the dashboard: heat is hard on old batteries.

## 9. Alarm clock, desk clock or digital photo frame

Simple and handy: leave it on the nightstand or shelf, plugged in, with an app that keeps the screen on or shows a slideshow.

## 10. Document scanner

With a scanning app, the phone becomes a portable scanner for receipts, contracts and papers. Send the scans to the file server so they do not stay lost in a gallery.

## How to choose

Choose what stays useful without you having to watch it. A camera, a panel and a clock need power and take up space. A file server works quietly in a drawer or on a shelf and solves a real problem: where to keep the home's photos, videos and documents without paying a monthly fee. To see if it fits your home, read [a home NAS without buying a NAS](/blog/home-nas-without-buying-a-nas/) and [traditional NAS or Android server](/blog/traditional-nas-vs-android-server/).

## Selling or recycling instead

If you would rather get rid of it, read [sell or reuse your old phone](/blog/sell-or-reuse-your-old-phone/) first: the right choice depends on the model, its condition and what is on it.

?? Is it safe to leave an old phone plugged in all day?
With a healthy battery, many people do. Keep it out of the sun and away from heat, and stop using it if the battery swells or the case lifts. Some phones get hot with the screen on all the time.

?? Can I use an old phone with no SIM card?
Yes. Everything here works over Wi-Fi except the hotspot, which needs a SIM with data.

?? Which old Android phone can be a file server?
One with Android 7.0 or newer that turns on, joins your Wi-Fi and has storage, or takes an SD card or a USB drive. Some makers limit background apps, so check the battery settings if the server stops.

?? Is a file server a backup?
No. Storage is not a backup. Keep an independent copy of the files you cannot lose.
